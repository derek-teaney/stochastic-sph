\title{The physical dimension of a stochastic run.\\ Plan for the fix (branch \texttt{dimfix})}
\author{D.~Teaney}
\affiliation{Department of Physics and Astronomy, Stony Brook University, Stony Brook, New York 11794, USA}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The code distinguishes the simulation dimension $d_s$, the number of directions the particles span, from the physical dimension $d$ of
the fluid.  A fluid that is independent of the unresolved directions makes sense for deterministic flow, but not with thermal
fluctuations: the noise, the statics and the long-time tails are all $d_s$-dimensional.  The stochastic runs so far had $d_s=2$ and
$d=3$, with $c_s^2=1/3$, so they simulated a two-dimensional fluid with $p=e/3$ and a bulk viscosity $\eta/3$.  This note states the rule,
lists where $d$ and $c_s^2$ enter the code, and plans the change, the tests and the reruns.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{The rule}

\paragraph{The point.}  With fluctuations, the fluid that is simulated is the fluid that the particles span.  The rule is
\begin{keyeq}
\st
\text{stochastic run (stochastic step or hybrid Monte Carlo):}\qquad d=d_s=2 .
\label{rule}
\stp
\end{keyeq}
A physical dimension $d\neq d_s$ (the $z$-independent fluid) is allowed only in deterministic runs.  One-dimensional stochastic runs are
dropped: $d_s=1$ remains for deterministic tests only.

\paragraph{The equation of state.}  The code uses an equation of state with a constant speed of sound,
\st
p=c_s^2\,e,\qquad T=T_{\rm scale}\,e^{c_s^2/(1+c_s^2)},\qquad s=\frac{e+p}{T},
\label{eos}
\stp
which is thermodynamically consistent for any $c_s^2$: it gives $ds/de=1/T$ and $dp/dT=s$.  It is a power law,
\st
p\propto T^{1+1/c_s^2},
\stp
so it is scale invariant for every $c_s^2$; that, not conformality, is what makes a constant $\eta/s$ natural.  It is conformal
(traceless) only when
\st
c_s^2=\frac1d .
\label{conformal}
\stp
The default becomes the conformal fluid in $d=2$, $c_s^2=1/2$, for which
\st
e=\left(\frac{T}{T_{\rm scale}}\right)^{3},\qquad s=\frac32\,\frac{T^2}{T_{\rm scale}^3} .
\stp
Other values of $c_s^2$ stay available as a parameter, named as such.

\paragraph{The bulk viscosity.}  The viscous and stochastic steps use the in-plane projector with $d_s=2$, and put the shear of the
$d-2$ unresolved directions into the bulk channel,
\st
\zeta_{\rm eff}=\zeta+2\eta\left(\frac12-\frac1d\right) .
\label{zetaeff}
\stp
With $d=3$ this is $\zeta+\eta/3$: the runs so far had a bulk viscosity $\eta/3$ although $\zeta=0$.  With $d=2$ it is $\zeta$, and the
conformal default has no bulk viscosity.  The formula stays in the code; only $d$ changes.

\section{What the runs so far simulated}
\label{sec:labels}

\paragraph{Labelling.}  The stochastic and hybrid Monte Carlo runs up to this branch are a consistent two-dimensional fluid,
\st
d_s=2,\qquad p=\frac{e}{3},\qquad \frac{\eta}{s}=\text{const},\qquad \zeta_{\rm eff}=\frac{\eta}{3},
\stp
not the intended conformal fluid.  They remain valid tests of the algorithm (detailed balance, the Hastings correction, the statics of
the positions) and are not deleted; the notes that report them (\texttt{fullruns.tex}, \texttt{inverse\_moment.tex},
\texttt{equilibrium.tex}, \texttt{statics.tex}) get one line saying so.  We are in the experimental period: old runs need not be
reproducible after this branch, and the final results will be rerun.

\paragraph{The statics.}  The Gaussian theory of the positions (\texttt{equilibrium.tex}) does not change form: $\alpha=(d_s+1)/2=3/2$
already counts the particle variables in $d_s=2$.  Its numbers change through the equation of state:
\st
\sigma=\frac{pV}{T}=\frac{c_s^2}{1+c_s^2}\,s V=\begin{cases}sV/4, & c_s^2=1/3,\\ sV/3, & c_s^2=1/2,\end{cases}
\stp
and the excess energy of a particle that sets the bath temperature,
\st
\Delta e=\frac{T}{2}\left[\frac{d\ln C_V}{d\ln T}+d_s\,\frac{d\ln\bigl(T(e+p)\bigr)}{d\ln T}\right]=
\begin{cases}\frac T2\,(3+2\times5), & c_s^2=1/3,\\[2pt] \frac T2\,(2+2\times4)=5T, & c_s^2=1/2 .\end{cases}
\stp

\section{The change}

The change is made in two commits, so that the restructuring can be checked against the old numerics before the numerics change.

\paragraph{Commit A: names and plumbing, numerics unchanged.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{ConformalEOS} $\to$ \texttt{ConstantCs2EOS} (the constant-speed-of-sound equation of state, \Eq{eos}), with the fields
  $c_s^2$, $T_{\rm scale}$, $\eta/s$, $\zeta/s$, and a constructor \texttt{conformal\_eos(d; T\_scale, eta\_by\_s, zeta\_by\_s)} with
  $c_s^2=1/d$.  A fast path for $c_s^2=1/2$ ($T\propto e^{1/3}$, \texttt{cbrt}).
\item \texttt{SimParams.d} $\to$ \texttt{phys\_dim}, beside \texttt{sim\_dim}.  The notes keep $d$ and $d_s$.
\item The drivers build the equation of state from the parameters (one helper instead of 49 literals), keeping $c_s^2=1/3$, $d=3$ for
  now.  Tests of the equation of state at a given $c_s^2$ keep their explicit values.
\item The longitudinal viscosity in \texttt{src/ideal.jl} written as $2\eta(1-1/d)+\zeta$.
\item Every run record (\texttt{.h5} attributes) gets \texttt{phys\_dim}, \texttt{cs2}, \texttt{T\_scale}, \texttt{eta\_by\_s},
  \texttt{zeta\_by\_s}; the analysis scripts read them instead of assuming $c_s^2=1/3$.
\end{enumerate}
Check: \texttt{test/run.sh all}, regression bit-identical against \texttt{statics}.

\paragraph{Commit B: the rule, numerics change.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{phys\_dim} defaults to \texttt{sim\_dim}; the drivers default to \texttt{conformal\_eos(phys\_dim)}, so $c_s^2=1/2$ and
  $\zeta_{\rm eff}=\zeta$.
\item A guard: the stochastic step and hybrid Monte Carlo throw an error if \texttt{phys\_dim} $\neq$ \texttt{sim\_dim} or if
  \texttt{sim\_dim} $=1$.
\item The \texttt{oneD} branches of \texttt{src/stochastic.jl} removed, and the one-dimensional modes of
  \texttt{examples/stochastic\_thermalize*.jl} and \texttt{examples/stochastic\_smallk.jl}.  One-dimensional deterministic code
  (\texttt{testgaussian\_1d}, \texttt{testsound\_viscous\_1d}, the rate bound) stays.
\item \texttt{Kappa3D}/\texttt{Kappa2D} moved to \texttt{src/legacy} with their two tests.
\end{enumerate}
Check: unit tests (new: thermodynamic consistency at $c_s^2=1/2$, $dp/dT=s$ and $ds/de=1/T$; the guard), every script loads, and a new
regression baseline for the stochastic paths.  Deterministic regressions run with $d=3$ given explicitly should stay bit-identical.

\section{Reruns}

\paragraph{Laptop.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The single-particle quadrature (\texttt{examples/single\_particle.jl}, normal and grid modes) at $c_s^2=1/2$: minutes.
\item The nine hybrid Monte Carlo runs of \texttt{equilibrium.tex} (\texttt{examples/volume\_fluctuations.jl}), the figures redone, and
  Appendix~B of \texttt{equilibrium.tex} rewritten with the new numbers.  The runs are labelled by $\sigma(T_0)$, the parameter of the
  theory, rather than by the entropy per particle of the lattice start.
\item A short stochastic run at rest, $\sigma\approx 15$: the sound speed rises from $1/\sqrt3$ to $1/\sqrt2$, so the time-step criterion,
  the residual acceptance and the light-cone rejections are checked again.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{NERSC.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \texttt{statics} campaign (\texttt{statics.tex}) on the fixed code; it has not been launched, so the fix costs no reruns there.
\item When the dynamics resumes: a small repeat of \texttt{dt\_scan} and the first Helfand case.  Older campaigns are left as they are,
  labelled as in \Sect{sec:labels}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Order.}  This note $\to$ commit A $\to$ commit B $\to$ laptop reruns $\to$ merge \texttt{dimfix} into \texttt{statics}
$\to$ the \texttt{statics} campaign.
