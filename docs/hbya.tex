\title{The kernel and the number of particles per kernel, $h/a$: campaign \texttt{hbya}}
\date{\today}
\maketitle

\section{Goals}

The $2\times2$ of \texttt{equilibrium\_positions.tex} (campaign \texttt{hmc\_liou2}: generalized leapfrog (GLF), Liouville measure, an equilibrium
HMC start, $h/a=2$, $s_a=541$) left three effects of the regulator:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Excess shear damping at rest.}  Every cell damps the shear mode faster than hydrodynamics,
  \st
  \frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{\rm hydro}}=1+\delta,\qquad \delta\approx0.10\quad (kh=0.26,\ 0.52),
  \stp
  with no sign of $\delta$ falling between $kh=0.26$ and $0.52$: more like a renormalisation of the viscosity at $h/a=2$ than a $(kh)^2$
  correction.
\item \emph{The regulator is not Lorentz invariant.}  At $v_0=0.8$ the excess is $\delta$ across the flow and $\gamma^2\delta$ along it, as for an
  extra dissipation fixed in the lab frame, and the pair correlation $g(r,\theta)$ is isotropic in the lab ($g_2,g_4,g_6\sim10^{-4}$) instead of
  contracted along the flow.
\item \emph{Finite entropy per particle.}  The HMC equilibrium has $C_{MM}/\chi_{MM}\approx1+8.5/s_a$, flat in $k$.  The hydrodynamics starts from
  the HMC equilibrium and can only be as good as it.
\end{enumerate}
The kernel is isotropic in the lab, so (2) remains at every $h/a$; but its size is set by $\delta$.  The questions for this campaign:
\begin{itemize}
\item Does $\delta$ fall as $h/a$ grows, and how fast?  Does it depend on the kernel (Wendland C2, C4, C6)?
\item Does the law $1+\gamma^2\delta$ along and $1+\delta$ across the flow hold at each $h/a$ and kernel?
\item Do the sound modes (frequency and damping) and the equal-time correlators $C_{AA}(\bm k,0)/\chi_{AA}$ improve with $h/a$?
\end{itemize}

\section{Design}

\paragraph{Fixed entropy per particle, fixed spacing.}  The spacing $a$ and the entropy per particle $s_a$ are held fixed and $h$ is raised:
\st
a=0.025,\qquad h=(h/a)\,a,\qquad s_a=S_0\,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}a^2=135\quad(S_0=2.5\times10^5),\qquad L=1.2 .
\stp
The number of particles is then fixed ($N=2688$) and the temperature too ($T=57.2$); only the number of neighbours grows, as $(h/a)^2$.  The
modes of the box are $kh=2\pi n h/L$: $kh=0.26n$ at $h/a=2$, $0.39n$ at $3$, $0.52n$ at $4$.  The common mode is $kh=0.52$; at $h/a=3$ it lies
between $0.39$ and $0.79$.  The alternative, fixed $h$ and smaller $a$ (as \texttt{examples/equilibrium.jl} did before the option
\texttt{SR\_H}), costs $(h/a)^{4.7}$ instead of $(h/a)^3$ and changes $T$.

\paragraph{Entropy per particle.}  $s_a=135$ rather than the default $541$: the run is cheaper by $T\propto s_a^{1/3}$ (the damping times scale
with $T$), and the finite-$s_a$ offset of the initial state, $8.5/s_a\approx6\%$, is known and the same at every $h/a$.  At $s_a=54$ it would
be $16\%$, and HMC needs a much smaller step.

\paragraph{Method.}  GLF ideal step (\texttt{SPH\_IDEAL=generalized}), one-kernel viscous step, Liouville measure, $\eta/s=0.2$; HMC start of 200
updates with random trajectory lengths in $[L/2c_s,L/c_s]$ (\texttt{SR\_HMC\_TAUJIT=0.5}, step factor $0.35$); $v_0=0$ and $0.8$; independent seeds
per set.  The run length is set to the same number of shear damping times at $kh=0.52$ for every $h/a$: $t\propto h^2$.

\paragraph{Measurements.}  As in \texttt{equilibrium\_positions.tex}, \texttt{examples/boost\_analysis.jl}: the shear damping $\Gamma/\Gamma_{\rm hydro}$
(fits of the shear mode) along and across the flow, the sound modes ($C_{xx}$, $C_{EE}$ along and $C_{yy}$ across the flow) against hydrodynamics, the
equal-time $C_{AA}(\bm k,0)/\chi_{AA}$, $g(r,\theta)$ (\texttt{examples/g\_theta.jl}), and $C_{MM}/\chi$ of the initial state.

\subsection{$h/a=3$, $4$ (complete: 128 runs each)}

Wall time per run: $h/a=3$ about $2$ h (packed, 4 threads), $h/a=4$ $2$--$4$ h (packed, 4 threads).  At $v_0=0.8$ the kinetic energy of the
fluctuations is $17.46$--$17.55$ (predicted $17.6$), and $\Delta H/H=2$--$10\times10^{-5}$ at the end of every set.

\paragraph{Shear damping at rest}, $\Gamma/\Gamma_{\rm hydro}$, the weighted mean of $\bm k$ along $x$ and $y$ (32 runs per kernel), at the two
smallest wavevectors of each $h/a$ (\Fig{fig:summary}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\hline
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=2$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=3$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=4$} \\
$kh$ & $0.26$ & $0.52$ & $0.39$ & $0.79$ & $0.52$ & $1.05$ \\ \hline
C2 & $1.07(6)$ & $1.105(28)$ & $1.16(4)$ & $1.145(21)$ & $1.23(3)$ & $1.280(14)$ \\
C4 & $1.12(5)$ & $1.094(31)$ & $1.13(3)$ & $1.142(17)$ & $1.22(4)$ & $1.210(14)$ \\
C6 & $1.12(6)$ & $1.077(24)$ & $1.09(3)$ & $1.133(17)$ & $1.19(3)$ & $1.190(14)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The excess grows with $h/a$}, from $\delta\approx0.09$ at $h/a=2$ to $0.14$ at $3$ and $0.19$--$0.28$ at $4$: roughly $\delta\approx0.05\,h/a$, for every
  kernel.  Raising $h/a$ at fixed spacing makes the shear damping worse, not better.
\item \emph{At fixed $h/a$ it hardly depends on $kh$} ($h/a=4$: $1.23$ and $1.28$ at $kh=0.52$ and $1.05$ for C2; equal for C4, C6).  An extra damping
  $\propto k^2$ at fixed $h$, i.e.\ an extra viscosity, $\nu\to\nu(1+\delta)$, with
  \st
  \frac{\delta\nu}{\nu}\approx0.05\,\frac ha\qquad(a=0.025,\ s_a=135,\ T=57.2):
  \stp
  it grows with the kernel size $h$, not with the inverse number of particles per kernel.  An error of discreteness would fall as $h/a$
  grows; this one does not.  Something that carries momentum over a distance of order $h$ --- for example the thermal motion of the
  particles through the kernel --- would behave this way.  Not yet tested: whether $\delta$ depends on $h$ or on $h/a$ (vary $a$ at fixed $h$).
\item \emph{The kernel matters little}: C6 is lowest, by $0.03$--$0.09$ at $h/a=4$; C2 and C4 agree within the errors except at $h/a=4$, $kh=1.05$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{At $v_0=0.8$} (16 runs per kernel; the second wavevector, $kh=0.52$, $0.79$, $1.05$, where the errors are smallest; \Fig{fig:summary}, right):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\hline
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=2$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=3$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$h/a=4$} \\
 & along & across & along & across & along & across \\ \hline
C2 & $1.34(10)$ & $1.15(8)$ & $1.24(8)$ & $1.28(6)$ & $1.33(6)$ & $1.41(4)$ \\
C4 & $1.33(15)$ & $1.18(7)$ & $1.26(10)$ & $1.21(5)$ & $1.27(9)$ & $1.33(3)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Only at $h/a=2$ is the damping along the flow larger than across it, as $1+\gamma^2\delta$ against $1+\delta$.  At $h/a=3$ and $4$ the two are equal within
the errors and about $0.1$ above the value at rest; $1+\gamma^2\delta$ would give $1.4$ ($h/a=3$) and $1.6$--$1.8$ ($h/a=4$) along the flow.  The
lab-frame picture of \texttt{equilibrium\_positions.tex} does not carry over to larger $h/a$; with 16 runs the errors along the flow are
$\pm0.06$--$0.15$.

\paragraph{The pair correlation} at $v_0=0.8$ is isotropic in the lab at every $h/a$ ($g_n\lesssim4\times10^{-4}$; \Fig{fig:gtheta4}), as expected: the
kernel is isotropic in the lab whatever its size.  The correlation hole fills in as $h/a$ grows: $g(0.75a)=0.75$, $0.83$, $0.88$ at $h/a=2$, $3$, $4$.

\paragraph{Equal-time correlators.}  At rest the momentum correlators lie within $0.05$ of $1$ at every $h/a$ and $kh$, mostly within $2\sigma$.  $C_{EE}/\chi$ rises with $kh$:
$1.01$, $1.05$, $1.10$--$1.15$ at $kh=0.52$, $1.05$, $1.57$ ($h/a=4$), smallest for C6 --- the finite-$kh$ correction of \texttt{equilibrium\_positions.tex}.
Along the flow at $v_0=0.8$ the errors of 16 runs ($\pm0.05$--$0.15$) leave the values at $1$ within $1$--$2\sigma$.

\section{Summary}

At fixed spacing and entropy per particle, raising $h/a$ from $2$ to $4$ \emph{doubles} the excess shear damping, from about $10\%$ to about $20\%$,
for all three kernels; the excess behaves as an extra viscosity proportional to $h$.  Larger $h/a$ is therefore not the fix for the
damping.  It does fill in the correlation hole, and it removes the $\gamma^2$ enhancement along the flow at $v_0=0.8$, though the damping there
is still about $0.1$ above its value at rest.  Next: separate $h$ from $h/a$ (vary $a$ at fixed $h$), and find what carries the extra
momentum over the kernel.
